# Migra Design Reference Review

**Purpose:** Extract the strongest transferable ideas from the supplied website references and define a powerful light-theme direction for Migra.  
**Technology:** Next.js  
**Brand constraint:** Retain Migra's existing navy and teal logo colours. No dark-theme website.  
**Date:** 15 October 2026

## Executive direction

Migra should not look like a conventional immigration agency or a generic SaaS template. It should feel like a trustworthy migration intelligence product with the emotional warmth of a human service.

The recommended combination is:

- **Visaro:** proof-led migration storytelling
- **Reloca:** human warmth and end-to-end relocation framing
- **Arvio and Cloudhub:** restrained product clarity
- **Finsaro and OneFin:** dashboard-led demonstrations of capability
- **Calorie App and Studia:** friendly, interactive explanation of a sophisticated product
- **Auraform and Fluence:** modular feature storytelling with interface demonstrations
- **Overtake:** sharp positioning, confident typography, and measurable outcomes

The result should be a light, spacious, editorial product experience using white, mist, pale teal, and soft blue surfaces; deep navy typography; teal actions; selective photography; and realistic product UI.

## Review of every reference

### 1. [Visaro](https://visaro-wbs.framer.website/)

**What works**

- The hero establishes regulation, reach, and operating history before making the promise.
- “One dedicated advisor” turns a broad service into a tangible relationship.
- Live-case and approval cards make the operation feel active rather than theoretical.
- The “wrong move costs you months” section frames the cost of confusion clearly.
- The here-to-there visual is an elegant metaphor for guided migration.
- Case studies contain route, timing, profession, and outcome—not vague praise.
- Pathway cards expose processing time and other decision data.

**Use for Migra**

- Put a compact verification strip above or beneath the hero.
- Show a sample Migra match or active journey beside the hero copy.
- Build evidence-rich customer stories around starting profile, obstacles, support, time, and result.
- Use a “where you are / where you could go / what comes next” visual.

**Do not copy**

- Unsupported approval percentages or template-style live cases.
- The warmer orange palette; translate the composition into Migra navy and teal.

### 2. [VisaFlow](https://visaflow.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Warm editorial background makes the service feel approachable.
- Large type creates a calm, premium first impression.
- Service options are grouped by user intent rather than administrative department.
- The page moves naturally from promise to breadth, process, proof, and action.

**Use for Migra**

- Use generous typography and breathing room.
- Organize entry points around work, study, family, business, and uncertainty.
- Keep service descriptions plain and outcome-oriented.

**Do not copy**

- Widely letter-spaced buttons; they reduce readability.
- Generic “visa company” positioning.

### 3. [Overtake](https://overtake-wbs.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Exceptionally strong point of view: outcomes instead of activity.
- Oversized typography and split-line composition create energy without clutter.
- Metrics are integrated into the narrative instead of isolated in a vanity-stat row.
- Case studies state the problem, intervention, and measurable result.
- The visual system is playful but disciplined.

**Use for Migra**

- Give the homepage a strong thesis: clarity and realistic next steps, not promises.
- Use large editorial statements between product sections.
- Turn outcomes into evidence-rich stories.
- Introduce tasteful kinetic typography or number transitions.

**Do not copy**

- Aggressive agency tone or dark sections.
- Decorative metrics that Migra cannot substantiate.

### 4. [Reloca](https://reloca-template.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Speaks about the full human transition, not only the visa.
- Photography and language make relocation feel personal and hopeful.
- Services extend through housing, arrival, career, and settlement.
- Destination exploration feels natural within the journey.
- The design balances coloured cards with a quiet light canvas.

**Use for Migra**

- Frame approval as one milestone within a larger relocation journey.
- Include pre-departure and settlement in the product story.
- Use authentic West African customer and specialist photography.
- Borrow the soft, varied card surfaces using pale teal and blue.

**Do not copy**

- Generic destination imagery or unsupported approval claims.

### 5. [OneFin](https://onefin.framer.website/)

**What works**

- A broad platform becomes understandable through product categories.
- Interface mockups prove the product instead of describing it abstractly.
- Benefit sections alternate between narrative, UI, and evidence.
- Repeated product surfaces create the feeling of a complete operating system.

**Use for Migra**

- Demonstrate the assessment result, roadmap, document checklist, and case timeline.
- Use interface fragments to explain the ecosystem.
- Group functionality into Discover, Prepare, Apply, and Relocate.

**Adaptation**

- Rebuild its visual drama on white and pale brand surfaces rather than black.

### 6. [Finora](https://finoraapp.framer.ai/)

**What works**

- Restrained monochrome foundation gives product imagery authority.
- Feature storytelling moves from a broad promise into focused capabilities.
- Product demonstrations sit inside large, clean frames.
- Strong section labelling improves scanability.

**Use for Migra**

- Use small uppercase labels such as Assessment, Match, Roadmap, and Support.
- Keep product frames large enough to be understood.
- Maintain a quiet background so the Migra UI carries the colour.

### 7. [GreenVest](https://greenvest.framer.website/)

**What works**

- A vivid green accent creates memorability on a simple white canvas.
- Rounded buttons and cards feel approachable.
- Benefit statements are direct and accessible.
- Pricing and features are easy to scan.

**Use for Migra**

- Let teal be an intentional action and progress colour.
- Use pill-shaped status labels and progress indicators.
- Keep pricing highly scannable.

**Do not copy**

- Generic claims or repetitive feature cards.

### 8. [Arvio](https://arvio.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Extremely clear product-first hierarchy.
- Complex workflows are explained in three simple steps.
- Product screenshots sit alongside concise benefit copy.
- Sections use whitespace and light grey surfaces rather than decoration.
- Integrations appear as supporting evidence, not the main story.

**Use for Migra**

- This is one of the strongest structural references.
- Explain assessment-to-action as a simple staged workflow.
- Pair each major capability with a believable product UI.
- Use a restrained layout with navy text and teal actions.

### 9. [Auraform](https://auraform.framer.ai/)

**What works**

- Strong problem-first section before feature presentation.
- Modular UI cards explain many capabilities without feeling like a checklist.
- Use cases let different audiences recognise themselves.
- Motion and oversized rounded forms create a sophisticated product feel.

**Use for Migra**

- Introduce the pain of migration fragmentation visually.
- Build an asymmetric feature grid for match explanation, cost planning, document readiness, alerts, and expert review.
- Include role-based entry points for workers, students, and families.

**Do not copy**

- Excessive animation or decorative blobs that reduce credibility.

### 10. [Lumora](https://lumora-template.framer.ai/)

**What works**

- Excellent “show the product in the hero” composition.
- Demonstrates answers grounded in named sources.
- The three-step flow is clear and specific.
- Connects features to decisions and actions rather than technology.
- UI copy feels calm and intelligent.

**Use for Migra**

- Show an assessment answer with reasons and official sources in the hero.
- Visually distinguish facts, sources, unknowns, and recommended actions.
- Use “from information to action” as a structural principle.

**Adaptation**

- Translate the sophisticated dark product treatment into a white and mist-blue interface.

### 11. [Flexfolio](https://flexfollio.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Friendly visual rhythm and varied card layouts.
- Social proof appears early.
- Feature sections have clear labels and concise descriptions.
- Rounded cards give the experience energy without requiring heavy illustration.

**Use for Migra**

- Use varied but systematic card sizes.
- Place credible social proof near the assessment entry.
- Build a modular homepage that can evolve as the product grows.

### 12. [Cloudhub](https://cloudhub-saas.framer.website/)

**What works**

- One of the clearest hero promises in the reference set.
- Every section demonstrates one capability with one product example.
- The writing is conversational and specific.
- Testimonials are detailed and tied to real usage.
- The visual system is minimal, light, and product-led.

**Use for Migra**

- Adopt its discipline: one section, one idea, one proof.
- Avoid crowded feature grids where a focused demonstration is stronger.
- Use customer stories that describe the before-and-after workflow.

### 13. [SaaSland](https://saasland.framer.media/)

**What works**

- Strong dashboard-led hero.
- Features, integrations, workflow, metrics, and pricing form a complete product narrative.
- The interface makes automation feel tangible.

**Use for Migra**

- Treat the Migra result dashboard as the main visual asset.

### 14. [Feature](https://feature.framer.website/)

### 15. [Nextar](https://nextar.framer.ai/)

**Use for Migra**

### 18. [Consumo](https://consumo-template.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Dashboard modules are integrated directly into benefit sections.

- Operational states and progress are visually clear.

**Use for Migra**

- Use large typography for the major strategic claims.

### 19. [Landio](https://landio.framer.website/)

### 20. [Imagio](https://imagio.framer.website/)

- “How it works” uses numbered, visual steps.

**Use for Migra**

- Consider a restrained serif for selected editorial headlines, paired with a highly legible sans-serif UI font.

### 21. [Fluence](https://fluence.framer.website/)

- Explain matching as profile signals → pathway evidence → next action.

### 22. [Calorie App](https://calories.framer.website/)

**What works**

- A single product interaction is made instantly understandable in the hero.
- Device imagery and floating data cards explain value without long text.
- Playful 3D icons turn an analytical product into something inviting.
- Interactive yes/no states make recommendations feel immediate.

**Use for Migra**

- Show a simple profile-to-pathway interaction in the hero.
- Use tasteful 3D or dimensional icons for goals, documents, money, language, and family.

### 23. [Studia](https://oma-studia.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Turns a complex AI capability into a simple input-output story.
- Uses an approachable product tone suited to people under cognitive load.
- Demonstrates the transformation of raw material into a structured learning journey.
- Product UI and content examples carry more weight than marketing claims.

**Use for Migra**

- Visualize transformation from scattered profile information into a clear migration plan.
- Make the assessment feel supportive, not like an examination.
- Use plain-language summaries with the option to inspect the evidence beneath them.

### 24. [Outside the Box](https://outsidetheboxtemplate.framer.website/)

**What works**

- Conversational product storytelling feels more like using the service than reading about it.
- Bold, unconventional composition creates memorability.
- The interface itself becomes the brand expression.
- Strong contrast between large statements and compact interactive moments.

**Use for Migra**

- Let visitors try a lightweight guided question in the homepage rather than only viewing a button.
- Use conversational microcopy during assessment.
- Create one surprising, ownable interaction—such as a live pathway-preview card that updates with the selected goal.

**Do not copy**

- Experimental composition that harms clarity, accessibility, or trust.

## Recommended Migra visual direction

### Creative idea

**Clarity in motion**

Migra should feel like complexity becoming organized. The visual language should move from scattered facts and uncertainty into structured matches, milestones, and confident next steps.

### Colour system

Use the current logo as the source of truth and derive production tokens after sampling the actual asset.

Suggested roles:

- **Migra navy:** primary text, primary buttons, footer, important data
- **Migra teal:** progress, positive status, links, active states, selected options
- **White:** dominant page canvas
- **Mist blue:** product frames and information sections
- **Pale teal:** matching, readiness, and success surfaces
- **Warm off-white:** selected human-story and editorial sections
- **Soft amber:** warnings and preparation gaps
- **Soft red:** blockers and urgent risks only

The site should remain unmistakably light. Navy may appear in contained components or the footer, but not as the dominant page background.

### Typography

- Use a modern grotesk or humanist sans-serif for navigation, body, data, and UI.
- Consider a restrained editorial display face for selected headlines if it improves warmth.
- Prefer large, concise headlines with compact line lengths.
- Avoid all-uppercase paragraphs and exaggerated letter spacing.

### Surfaces

- White page background with alternating pale blue, pale teal, and warm-neutral sections
- Product cards with 16–24px radii
- Thin neutral borders and very soft shadows
- Pills for status, category, and source freshness
- Large product frames rather than many tiny screenshots
- Authentic photography in a limited number of emotionally important sections

### Motion

- Animate pathway cards into a ranked order after sample answers change.
- Use subtle progress transitions for assessment steps.
- Reveal source and reasoning layers smoothly.
- Animate roadmap milestones along a single journey line.
- Keep movement short, purposeful, and reduced-motion compatible.
- Avoid endless marquees, floating decoration, and motion that competes with reading.

## Next.js implementation direction

### Recommended foundation

- Next.js App Router
- TypeScript
- Server Components by default
- Client Components only for assessment, comparison, filters, and interactive demonstrations
- Tailwind CSS or a token-driven CSS layer
- Accessible primitives such as Radix UI where useful
- Motion library such as Motion for React, used selectively
- Headless content source for pathways, countries, guides, and updates
- Structured metadata and schema markup for pathways, articles, FAQs, and organization data

### Component system

- `SiteHeader`
- `HeroAssessmentPreview`
- `TrustStrip`
- `GoalSelector`
- `ProblemTransformation`
- `JourneySteps`
- `PathwayMatchCard`
- `MatchReasonList`
- `SourceFreshnessBadge`
- `PathwayComparison`
- `ReadinessRoadmap`
- `DocumentChecklistPreview`
- `SpecialistProfileCard`
- `SupportedPathwayGrid`
- `CustomerJourneyStory`
- `PricingPlans`
- `ResourceCard`
- `FinalCTA`

### Performance requirements

- Optimize and responsively size every image.
- Prefer CSS and SVG over video for decorative effects.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold product previews.
- Keep hero interaction lightweight.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Use route-level loading states and progressive enhancement.
- Establish performance budgets for JavaScript, images, and fonts.

### Accessibility requirements

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and interaction standards.
- Ensure the assessment is fully keyboard operable.
- Do not communicate fit using colour alone.
- Provide plain-language error and uncertainty states.
- Use readable type sizes and generous touch targets.
- Ensure motion can be reduced.
- Make sources and definitions available without tooltips alone.

## Final recommendation

The strongest creative direction is not to select one template and recolour it. Migra should combine:

- Visaro's proof-led migration structure
- Reloca's human and settlement-focused warmth
- Arvio and Cloudhub's clarity and restraint
- Finsaro's light dashboard aesthetic
- Lumora's evidence-grounded product demonstration
- Calorie App's approachable interaction
- Overtake's confidence and editorial rhythm

The homepage should make the product usable before asking for a conversation. Its defining visual should be an interactive, explainable pathway result—not a stock photograph, globe, airplane, or generic dashboard.
